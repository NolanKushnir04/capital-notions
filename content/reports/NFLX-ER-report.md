## 1. Executive summary

We initiate coverage of Netflix (NASDAQ: NFLX) with a Buy rating and a 12-month price target of $71.11, 19.1% above the 17 November 2026 price of $59.70. The shares fell 46% over the past year, while consensus 2027 EPS fell only 2.5% between May and November. Our estimates sit within 1–3% of consensus. We see the opportunity in valuation: the market is pricing a weaker business than the forecasts describe.

**Thesis:** Netflix trades at 15.9x our 2027 EPS, a 19% discount to the peer median of 19.5x. We forecast revenue growth of about 9% a year through 2030 and EPS growth of 17% a year from 2027. Pricing power is intact: recent US price rises landed as expected, and churn improved year on year in the third quarter. Content costs grow slower than revenue, lifting operating margin from 31.5% in 2026 to 35.9% in 2030. The 2026 advertising miss reflects delayed product rollouts, not weaker demand. Our target assumes $6.9B of ad revenue by 2030, 32% below Bloomberg Intelligence. It does not need Netflix to reach its own goals.

**Forecasts:** We forecast revenue of $56.2B in 2027 (+10.0%), rising to $70.2B in 2030. Diluted EPS rises from $3.76 in 2027 to $6.00 in 2030. Free cash flow grows from $12.0B in 2026 to $19.9B in 2030. We assume 95% of free cash flow funds buybacks, cutting the share count 22% by 2030. For 2027, our revenue is 0.5% below consensus and our EPS 0.9% below.

**Valuation and scenarios:** Our target blends a DCF (50%) with peer P/E (25%) and peer EV/EBITDA (25%) multiples. The three give $65.41, $73.39 and $54.28 per share, for an intrinsic value of $64.62 today. We roll that forward one year at our 10.0% cost of equity to reach $71.11. Our DCF uses a 9.7% WACC and 3.5% terminal growth. The current price implies only 2.8% terminal growth on our cash flows. Our bull case gives $80.93 (+36%), with ad revenue reaching about $9.1B by 2030 and US price rises near their recent pace. Our bear case gives $57.88 (−3%), with continued engagement losses, slower pricing and higher content costs. Weighting the scenarios 25/50/25 gives $70.26 (+18%). We see the risk-reward as skewed to the upside.

## 2. Investment thesis

We rate Netflix a Buy with a $71.11 price target, 19% above the current $59.70. The shares fell 46% over the past year, but consensus 2027 EPS fell only 2.5% between May and November. Our own estimates sit within 1–3% of consensus. We think the market is pricing a weaker business than the forecasts describe. Our thesis rests on three points.

### 1. The stock has de-rated while earnings estimates have held

Netflix trades at 15.9x our 2027 EPS of $3.76, against a peer median of 19.5x. That is a 19% discount for a company we expect to grow revenue about 9% a year through 2030 and EPS 17% a year from 2027. The discount opened over 2026 as the share price fell, not as forecasts fell. Consensus 2027 revenue is down only 1.4% since May. Our DCF shows what the current price assumes: with our cash flows, $59.70 implies only 2.8% long-term growth, or a 10.3% WACC. We use 3.5% and 9.7%. Closing that gap is worth $4.92 per share. Holding the stock for 12 months at a fair value adds a further $6.49 at our 10.0% cost of equity. Together, those make up the $11.41 of upside to our target.

### 2. Pricing power and cost discipline still drive earnings growth

Netflix's recent price rises, including in the US, landed in line with past increases, according to management. Third-quarter churn improved year on year, both globally and in the US. We forecast ARM growing 5–8% a year in UCAN on price increases of 4–6% plus advertising. Content amortization grows 1–3 points slower than revenue each year. Operating margin widens from 31.5% in 2026 to 35.9% in 2030. Free cash flow rises from $12.0B to $19.9B. We assume 95% of it funds buybacks, cutting the share count by 22% by 2030. That turns about 8% annual revenue growth into 17% annual EPS growth from 2027 to 2030.

### 3. The advertising miss is about timing, and our target does not depend on ads

Netflix cut its 2026 ad revenue target by about $300M, to $2.7B. Ad revenue is still growing more than 80% this year. Management attributed the shortfall to delays in rolling out new ad products and partner integrations, not to weaker demand. Deutsche Bank reached the same conclusion. Demand indicators remain strong: Bloomberg Intelligence reports that Netflix ads reach more than 250M monthly active viewers and about 75M ad-tier members, and that US Upfront commitments nearly doubled. We take the lower base and forecast $6.9B of ad revenue by 2030. That is 32% below Bloomberg Intelligence's $10.1B and below Netflix's reported internal goal of about $9B. If Netflix reaches Bloomberg Intelligence's path, our bull case gives $80.93 per share.

### What would change our view

Engagement is the main debate, and management accepts it is not where it should be. The CFO said on the third-quarter call that Netflix wants to grow view hours faster and is "not satisfied." We would revisit our rating on three signals. The first is US viewing share falling further on Nielsen data. The second is 2027 revenue guidance below 10%. The third is a 2027 margin target below 33%. Our bear case, at $57.88, captures that path. It sits 3% below the current price, against 36% upside in our bull case.

## 3. Catalysts

The next major catalyst is fourth-quarter results on 20 January 2027, when Netflix gives its first formal 2027 guidance. Beyond that, the catalysts are evidence on engagement, advertising execution and the new content leadership. We list them in date order.

**Fourth-quarter 2026 results and 2027 guidance (20 January 2027).** Management's stated objective is sustained double-digit revenue growth, rising margins and rising free cash flow. Our 2027 forecasts are 10.0% revenue growth and a 33.3% operating margin. Consensus is 10.6% and 33.2%. Guidance at or above those levels would confirm that estimates are holding. We would also look for a 2027 ad revenue target. Consensus fourth-quarter figures match guidance, so the market expects no beat.

**Fourth-quarter content slate (November 2026 to January 2027).** The US slate includes the limited series East of Eden and season four of The Diplomat. Nielsen's monthly viewing-share data will show whether these titles slow YouTube's share gains. Netflix's share of US TV time was 7.7% in August 2026, down from 8.7% a year earlier.

**Advertising rollouts and the 2027 Upfront (first half of 2027).** The delayed ad products and partner integrations are due to come online through 2027. The annual Upfront, where advertisers commit annual budgets, typically takes place in May. Bloomberg Intelligence reports that US Upfront commitments nearly doubled last year. A similar result would support our $3.9B 2027 ad forecast and point toward the bull case.

**First content decisions under Peter Rice (2027).** Netflix appointed Peter Rice to lead content in 2026, after the previous chief content officer left. Co-CEO Ted Sarandos said the US needs better execution, not a change of strategy. Greenlights and the 2027 slate will show whether US output improves.

**Pricing (2027).** Our base case assumes a US price increase of about 6% in 2027, in effect for half the year. A timely increase that does not raise churn would confirm that pricing power is intact. A delay would point toward our bear case.

**Buybacks (ongoing).** Netflix had $22.3B of buyback authorization left after the third quarter. Its $4.8B of third-quarter buybacks were the largest in its history. Buying at today's price retires more shares per dollar than our model assumes, which lifts EPS.

## 4. Company overview

Netflix is the world's largest subscription streaming service, with about 325M paid memberships in over 190 countries. FY2025 revenue was $45.2B, up 16%. Netflix reports one segment, streaming, and earns about 97% of revenue from monthly subscription fees. Advertising was about 3% of revenue in 2025 and is guided to about 5% in 2026.

### Business model

Netflix sells access to one global library of series, films, games and live events. Revenue equals paid memberships multiplied by average monthly revenue per membership (ARM). In the US, plans cost $8.99 (Standard with ads), $19.99 (Standard) and $26.99 (Premium) after the March 2026 price increase. Members are billed monthly, so revenue is recurring and highly predictable.

Content is the main cost. Content amortization was $16.4B in FY2025, equal to 36% of revenue and about 70% of cost of revenues. Because content is largely a fixed cost spread across a growing global base, revenue growth turns into margin expansion. Operating margin rose from 17.8% in FY2022 to 29.5% in FY2025.

### Revenue by region

US and Canada (UCAN) is the largest region, with FY2025 revenue of $20.0B (44% of total, +15.0%). Growth there now comes mainly from price increases on a mature membership base. Europe, Middle East and Africa (EMEA) generated $14.5B (32%, +17.2%), from both member growth and pricing.

Latin America (LATAM) generated $5.4B (12%, +10.7%). Local prices rise often, in line with inflation, but weaker currencies give back much of that growth in US dollars. Asia-Pacific (APAC) also generated $5.4B (12%) and grew fastest, at +21.3%, led by new members in India.

### History

Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph founded Netflix in 1997 as a DVD-by-mail service. Netflix launched its monthly subscription model in 1999 and went public in 2002 at $15 per share. It turned its first profit in 2003, the year it passed 1M subscribers.

Netflix launched streaming in 2007 and signed licensing deals with the major studios over the following six years. International expansion began with Canada in 2010, followed by the UK, Ireland and the Nordics in 2012. Its first major original series arrived in 2013, and in 2016 it launched in 130 more countries at once.

The past five years reshaped the business model. Netflix launched an ad-supported plan in 2022 and began its paid-sharing crackdown in 2023, converting borrowers into paying members. In 2025 it stopped reporting memberships quarterly and completed a 10-for-1 stock split.

In December 2025, Netflix agreed to buy Warner Bros.' studios and HBO Max at an enterprise value of $82.7B. It walked away in February 2026, when Warner Bros. Discovery accepted Paramount Skydance's higher bid, and collected a $2.8B termination fee. Netflix raised US prices by about 10% on average in March 2026. In October 2026 it appointed new content leadership under Peter Rice and announced a headcount reduction.

### Management and governance

Netflix is led by co-CEOs Ted Sarandos (content) and Greg Peters (product and business), with Spencer Neumann as CFO. Co-founder Reed Hastings, CEO until 2023, left the board after the annual shareholder meeting on 4 June 2026. Jay Hoag, a director since 1999 and a founding general partner of TCV, replaced him as chair. Netflix has a single share class, so voting power matches economic ownership.

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly. Netflix returned about 96% of FY2025 free cash flow through $9.1B of buybacks, and repurchased a record $4.8B in Q3 2026. It walked away from the Warner Bros. deal rather than raise its bid. Net debt was 0.4x EBITDA at end-2025, and Moody's upgraded Netflix to A2 in June 2026.

### Ownership

Institutions own 87.4% of shares outstanding. The five largest holders own about 30%: Vanguard (9.9%), BlackRock (8.5%), State Street (4.4%), Capital Group (3.9%) and FMR (3.4%). Short interest is low at 2.1% of shares, or 2.5 days of trading volume. Insider selling is routine: co-CEO Ted Sarandos sells under a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan adopted on 4 May 2026.

## 5. Industry and competitive landscape

Streaming has become the core of the TV business, and Netflix is its largest player. In 2025, online video revenue overtook pay TV for the first time, rising 13.5% to $176B while pay TV fell 4% to $170B (Omdia). The industry has moved from a race for subscribers to a contest for viewing time and monetization: price increases, ad tiers, bundles and live events.

### Market size

Global subscription streaming revenue reached $157.1B in 2025, up 14% and triple the roughly $50B of 2020 (Ampere Analysis). Growth is slowing as markets mature: Ampere expects revenue to rise 29% over five years to more than $200B by 2030, or about 5% a year. Including advertising, total streaming revenue was $177B in 2025 (Ampere).

Netflix's own view of its runway is wider than streaming alone. On the Q1 2026 call, co-CEO Greg Peters put Netflix's addressable market at $670B and said Netflix accounts for only about 5% of TV viewing globally. On the Q2 2026 call, management said Netflix is less than 45% penetrated in roughly 800M addressable households and captures about 7% of addressable revenue.

That leaves three growth levers: more households (mostly APAC, LATAM and parts of EMEA), higher revenue per household (mostly UCAN and Western Europe), and advertising.

### Industry structure and trends

Consolidation is concentrating content ownership. Paramount Skydance's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, agreed in February 2026 for about $111B, combines Paramount+ and HBO Max. A small group of large studios (Disney, Comcast, Paramount-WBD, Sony and Amazon) now owns most new and library content, and licenses less of it to Netflix.

Prices are rising across the industry. Disney+ raised its ad tier from $9.99 to $11.99 a month and its ad-free plan from $15.99 to $18.99. Netflix's ad plan, at $8.99, is still cheaper than Disney+'s. Ad tiers widen reach and add a second revenue stream: more than 60% of Netflix sign-ups in ad markets chose the ad plan in the first quarter of 2026 (Q1 2026 shareholder letter).

Bundling lowers the cost of switching. Amazon includes Prime Video with a Prime membership, and Disney bundles Disney+, Hulu and ESPN. Netflix has answered with live events, adding NFL games and WWE, which lift engagement and command premium ad prices.

Competition is increasingly for attention rather than subscriptions. YouTube is now Netflix's largest competitor for TV-screen time and ad dollars, and short-form video competes for younger viewers. Netflix's viewing hours grew only about 2% in H1 2026, to more than 97B hours (Netflix engagement report, July 2026).

Labor relations add uncertainty. The 2023 writers' and actors' strikes halted production for months, and the settlements did not resolve how AI can be used in production. Further disputes could delay releases and raise costs.

### Competitors

Alphabet's YouTube (Alphabet market cap: about $3.5T) is Netflix's main rival for viewing time. It has free, user-generated scale, the largest share of TV viewing and the leading video ad business, but little premium scripted content. Consensus expects Alphabet's revenue to grow 31.3% in FY2027, driven mostly by AI and cloud rather than YouTube.

Walt Disney (about $202B) competes through Disney+, Hulu and ESPN. It is stronger in franchise IP (Marvel, Star Wars, Pixar), sports and bundling, but has a smaller library and much lower streaming margins. Consensus expects 4.8% revenue growth in its fiscal year to September 2027.

Amazon (about $2.9T) includes Prime Video with Prime and has deep pockets, but video is a perk of Prime rather than a profit center. Paramount Skydance (about $40B) will own HBO's premium library once the WBD deal closes, but carries heavy debt, integration risk and loss-making streaming. Its 35.7% FY2027E growth reflects the acquisition, not organic growth.

Spotify (about $124B; 14.8% FY2027E growth) is a different medium but a useful model of a combined subscription and advertising business. Netflix's market cap is about $245B at $59.70, with FY2027E consensus revenue growth of 10.6%.

### Market share and competitive position

Netflix is the largest streaming business by revenue. Its FY2025 subscription revenue of about $43.7B equals roughly 28% of the $157.1B global total (CN calculation). In the US, where rivals are strongest, JustWatch data put Prime Video at 21% of the subscription streaming market in Q1–Q3 2025, Netflix at 20%, Disney+ and HBO Max at 13% each and Hulu at 11%. In Q4 2025, Netflix moved narrowly ahead, with 20% against Prime Video's 19%.

Netflix's advantages are scale and data: a global library in more than 50 languages, recommendation technology built on two decades of viewing data, and a content budget of about $20B in 2026, spread across more members than any rival. Rival consolidation will narrow the gap in the US. JustWatch estimates that a combined Paramount and WBD would hold about 20% of the US market, level with Netflix and just behind Prime Video at 21%.

The main competitive question is no longer whether rivals can match Netflix's subscriptions. It is whether Netflix can hold its share of viewing time against YouTube and short-form video. Management said on the Q3'26 call that it is "not satisfied" with view-hour growth, which is the central debate in Section 8 (Risks).

## 6. Financial analysis and forecasts

We forecast revenue growth slowing from 13.2% in 2026 to 6.9% by 2030, while operating margin widens from 31.5% to 35.9%. Revenue reaches $70.2B in 2030, a 9.2% compound annual growth rate from 2025. Diluted EPS rises from $2.53 in 2025 to $6.00 in 2030, a 19% annual rate. Margin expansion and buybacks drive that EPS growth more than revenue does. Our estimates sit within 1–3% of consensus for 2026–2028, so our Buy call rests on valuation, not on out-forecasting the Street.

### 2026 is anchored to actual results

Our 2026 figures combine nine months of reported results with management's fourth-quarter guidance. That gives revenue of $51.1B (+13.2%), operating income of $16.1B and an operating margin of 31.5%, all in line with guidance. Reported 2026 EPS of $3.62 includes the one-off $2.8B Warner Bros. Discovery termination fee, worth about $0.55 per share after tax. Excluding the fee, 2026 EPS is about $3.07. That makes our 2027 EPS of $3.76 roughly 22% growth on an underlying basis.

### Revenue: price and advertising take over from member growth

We build revenue region by region, as paid members multiplied by average revenue per member (ARM), adjusted for currency. Netflix stopped reporting membership numbers after 2024, so our member figures are CN estimates. We estimate members grow from about 325M in 2025 to 397M in 2030, or 4.1% a year. Blended ARM rises from $12.02 to $14.99 a month over the same period.

UCAN is a pricing story. We assume members grow 1% a year and ARM grows 5–8%. ARM growth comes from price increases of 4–6% a year, plus advertising. UCAN revenue growth slows from 8.8% in 2027 to 6.4% by 2030.

EMEA grows 10.7% in 2027, slowing to 7.1% by 2030. Member growth of 4% a year eases to 2.5%, and ARM grows 4–6%.

APAC is the fastest-growing region. Members grow 10% in 2027, easing to 7% by 2030, while ARM rises only about 2% a year because cheaper plans dominate. Revenue growth slows from 13.5% to 9.2%, and APAC's share of revenue rises from 11.9% to 13.3%.

LATAM has the strongest local-currency growth but the weakest reported growth. We deduct 3.5 points a year for currency weakness, so reported growth slows from 8.8% to 5.5%. LATAM is the only region where we forecast a currency drag after 2026. Currency is already a large swing factor there: it cut LATAM's 2025 reported growth by 9.6 points.

### Advertising: we sit below the Street and below management

We forecast advertising revenue rising from $2.7B in 2026 to $6.9B in 2030, or 9.8% of total revenue. Growth slows from 44% in 2027 to 15% in 2030. Our 2030 figure is about 32% below Bloomberg Intelligence's $10.1B forecast. It is also below the roughly $9B internal goal reported by The Wall Street Journal. We start from the reduced 2026 guidance of $2.7B (cut from $3.0B), because management has already missed its own ad target once this year. Ads are included in regional revenue, not added on top. Hitting the Bloomberg Intelligence path is the main driver of our bull case (Section 7).

### Margins: content costs grow slower than revenue

Operating margin expands about 1 point a year, from 31.5% in 2026 to 35.9% in 2030. Most of the gain comes from content. We grow content amortization 7.0% in 2027, easing to 5.5% by 2030, 1–3 points below revenue growth each year. Content amortization falls from 36.3% of revenue in 2025 to 32.5% in 2030. That continues the pattern of 2023–2025, when amortization grew 5.4% a year against revenue growth of 12.7%. Live sports, about 5% of 2026 content spend, limit how far the ratio can fall.

Operating expenses fall from 19.0% of revenue in 2025 to 17.6% in 2030. General and administrative costs drop to 4.0% of revenue in 2027 after the headcount reduction announced in October 2026, the largest since 2022. Technology and development stays near 7.5–7.8% because of continued spending on ad technology and games. Gross margin rises from 48.5% in 2025 to 53.5% in 2030.

### Earnings: buybacks add about 6 points a year to EPS growth

We forecast net income of $15.1B in 2027, rising to $20.5B in 2030. We hold the effective tax rate at 17%, in line with the nine-month 2026 rate of 17.3%. That rate is above the 12–14% of 2021–2025 because the 15% global minimum tax now applies. Diluted shares fall from 4,344M in 2025 to 3,408M in 2030, a 22% reduction. As a result, EPS grows 16.9% a year from 2027 to 2030, against net income growth of about 11%. We set the future buyback price at today's share price compounded at the cost of equity. If the stock stays cheaper than that, buybacks would retire more shares and lift EPS further.

### Cash flow: free cash flow doubles by 2030

We forecast free cash flow of $12.0B in 2026, in line with guidance, rising to $13.5B in 2027 and $19.9B in 2030. Free cash flow margin widens from 20.9% in 2025 to 28.4% in 2030. Cash content spending runs at 1.10x amortization in 2026–2027, in line with management's guidance, and eases to 1.05x by 2030. Capital spending peaks at 1.8% of revenue in 2026 as studio projects are completed, then falls to 1.5%.

### Capital allocation: about $64B of buybacks over 2027–2030

2026 buybacks total $13.5B, above free cash flow, of which $10.8B was completed in the first nine months. From 2027 we assume Netflix returns 95% of free cash flow through buybacks, in line with the 87–97% of 2023–2025, or $63.9B across 2027–2030. We assume each debt maturity is refinanced at a 5.5% interest rate, so gross debt stays near $15.2B. That keeps Netflix inside its commitment to a solid investment-grade rating (Moody's A2, S&P A). Cash rises from $7.8B at the end of 2026 to $12.1B in 2030. Netflix had $22.3B of buyback authorization remaining after the third quarter of 2026.

### CN vs consensus

Our estimates sit close to the Street's. For 2027, our revenue of $56.2B is 0.5% below consensus, operating income 0.2% below and EPS of $3.76 0.9% below. For 2028, our revenue is 1.3% below consensus, operating income 2.7% below and EPS of $4.43 1.5% below. In 2027 the gap is almost all below operating income. Our 17% tax rate and $225M of other income cut net income 2.9% below consensus. A lower share count recovers most of that at the EPS line. By 2028 we are slightly more cautious on margin as well: 34.3% against the Street's 34.8%. Because we do not forecast an earnings beat, the upside to our target comes from the stock's valuation.

## 7. Valuation

We set a 12-month price target of $71.11, 19.1% above the 17 November 2026 price of $59.70, and rate Netflix a Buy. The target blends a discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation with two peer multiples, giving an intrinsic value of $64.62 per share today. We roll that forward one year at our 10.0% cost of equity to reach $71.11. Our scenarios range from $57.88 (bear) to $80.93 (bull). The probability-weighted value of $70.26 also supports a Buy.

### Method and weights

We weight the DCF at 50%, peer P/E at 25% and peer EV/EBITDA at 25%. The DCF gets the largest weight because it is the only method built on our own forecasts. The two multiples check that result against how the market prices comparable companies. The DCF gives $65.41 per share, contributing $32.70. Peer P/E gives $73.39, contributing $18.35. Peer EV/EBITDA gives $54.28, contributing $13.57. Together they sum to $64.62.

### Cost of capital: WACC of 9.7%

We calculate a weighted average cost of capital (WACC) of 9.73%. Our cost of equity is 10.04%, from the capital asset pricing model. We use a 4.88% risk-free rate, the 10-year US Treasury yield. We use a 4.41% equity risk premium, Damodaran's implied premium for the S&P 500. Our beta of 1.17 averages two estimates. The first is Netflix's own regression beta: raw 1.61, adjusted 1.41 (Bloomberg, five years of monthly returns). The second is a bottom-up beta of 0.94, built from entertainment and entertainment-software peers. We blend the two because the regression explains only 30% of Netflix's share price moves (R² of 0.30).

Our pre-tax cost of debt is 5.66%, the yield on Netflix's 5.25% bond due 2036. That is 4.70% after tax. Debt is only 5.8% of capital at market values, so it barely moves the WACC.

Bloomberg's own WACC for Netflix is higher, at 12.1%. The gap comes from Bloomberg's 8.1% equity risk premium, which adds a country-risk component. At an 11.2% WACC, our DCF value falls to $52.30 (Exhibit 11).

### DCF: $65.41 per share

We discount unlevered free cash flow for 2027–2030, using mid-year discounting, with a terminal value at the end of 2030. Free cash flow treats cash content spending as Netflix's real capital spending. Stock-based compensation is treated as a cost, not added back. Unlevered free cash flow rises from $13.4B in 2027 to $19.6B in 2030, or 28% of revenue.

We assume 3.5% terminal growth, below the 4.88% risk-free rate. The terminal value equals 12.6x 2030 EBITDA, close to the 12.0x peer median on 2027 estimates and below Netflix's own 13.1x today, for a business still growing about 7% in 2030. That suggests our terminal assumption is not aggressive. The terminal value makes up 80% of enterprise value, which is typical for a company still growing at 7–10%.

Enterprise value is $279.5B at the end of 2026. Deducting $14.5B of debt and adding $7.8B of cash gives equity value of $272.8B. That is $66.17 per share on 4,123M diluted shares, or $65.41 discounted back to today.

### Peer multiples: cheap on earnings, fuller on EBITDA

We compare Netflix with six peers on Bloomberg consensus 2027 estimates. The peers are Disney, Spotify, Alphabet, Meta, Roku and Paramount Skydance. We use the median rather than the average because Alphabet's 31% revenue growth and Spotify's 32x EV/EBITDA skew the mean.

The peer median P/E is 19.5x. Netflix trades at 15.9x our 2027 EPS of $3.76, a 19% discount. Applying the peer median gives $73.39.

The peer median EV/EBITDA is 12.0x. Applying it to our 2027 EBITDA of $19.2B gives $54.28. EBITDA understates Netflix's cost base relative to peers, because content amortization, its largest cost, sits above the EBITDA line. That is why the EV/EBITDA value is the lowest of the three, and why it carries only 25% weight.

### Price target: $71.11

We roll the $64.62 intrinsic value forward one year at the 10.04% cost of equity, giving $71.11. The $11.41 of upside to the target has two parts. The first is $4.92: the gap between today's price and intrinsic value. The second is $6.49: the return a fairly priced stock should earn over 12 months. The target therefore needs no rise in valuation multiples beyond closing the current discount.

### Sensitivity

The DCF is most sensitive to WACC and terminal growth. A 0.5-point change in WACC moves the DCF value by about $5–6 per share. A 0.5-point change in terminal growth moves it by $4–5. Across WACC of 8.2–11.2% and terminal growth of 2.5–4.5%, the DCF ranges from $47.23 to $107.91. Using an exit multiple of 16x 2030 EBITDA instead of perpetuity growth gives $80.07.

### What the current price implies

The current price of $59.70 already accepts our forecasts at a lower valuation. With our cash flows unchanged, it implies terminal growth of about 2.8%, against our 3.5%. Alternatively, it implies a WACC of about 10.3%, against our 9.7%. The market is pricing in either slower long-term growth or a higher risk premium, not lower near-term earnings. Our estimates are within 1–3% of consensus, which supports that view.

### Scenarios

We model three scenarios using the same method, WACC and peer multiples, so the values are directly comparable. Only the business drivers and terminal growth change. 2026 is identical in all three because it is anchored to nine months of actual results.

In our bull case (25% probability), ad revenue grows at Bloomberg Intelligence's forecast rates from Netflix's lower 2026 base of $2.7B, reaching about $9.1B by 2030. That is below BI's $10.1B, which starts from a pre-cut $3.0B base, and in line with Netflix's reported internal goal of about $9B. US price increases continue near their recent pace (7% a year, easing to 5%), and a stronger US slate lifts membership growth. Terminal growth is 4.0%. Revenue reaches $75.2B in 2030, operating margin 38.0% and 2027 EPS $3.93. That gives a value of $80.93, 36% above the current price. Bull 2027 revenue matches the highest Street estimate. Bull 2030 operating income of $28.6B sits just below Netflix's reported internal target of about $30B.

Our base case (50% probability) gives our $71.11 price target, 19% above the current price.

In our bear case (25% probability), Netflix keeps losing viewing share and has to hold back on pricing and spend more on content. Netflix's share of US TV time fell from 8.7% to 7.7% in the year to August 2026. YouTube's rose to a record 15.8% (Nielsen). In the bear case, US price increases slow to 3–4% a year and US membership growth stops. Ad revenue reaches only $5.0B by 2030. Content amortization grows 10% in 2027. Terminal growth is 3.0%. Operating margin falls to 30.4% by 2030 and 2027 EPS to $3.45, giving a value of $57.88, 3% below the current price. That is close to the most bearish brokers: Wells Fargo at $57 and Huber Research at $60.

Weighting the scenarios 25/50/25 (our judgment) gives a value of $70.26, 18% above the current price. The bear case is 3% below the price and the bull case 36% above. We see the risk-reward as skewed to the upside.

### CN vs the Street

Our $71.11 target is 17% below the Bloomberg consensus target of $85.76. Of 63 analysts, 42 rate the stock Buy, 19 Hold and 2 Sell. Targets range from $57 to $126. Our earnings estimates match consensus, so the gap comes from valuation, mainly our 9.7% WACC and the 25% weight on EV/EBITDA. Recent targets cluster between $75 and $90. Our target sits just below that range, between Huber's $60 and BMO's $125. The Street has cut its consensus target by about 40% this year while keeping Buy ratings near 65–70%. After the third-quarter results, Huatai downgraded the stock to Hold ($69) and Morningstar upgraded it to Buy ($80). Huber Research cut its target to $60, and BMO kept its $125 target, near the Street high. We are a Buy, but more cautious than the Street on what the shares are worth.

## 8. Risks

The main risk to our Buy rating is a continued loss of viewing time, which would weaken pricing and push up content spending. That is our bear case, worth $57.88, or about $13 below our target. We rank the risks below by their potential impact on value.

**Engagement and competition from YouTube (high impact).** YouTube's share of US TV time rose to a record 15.8% in August 2026. Netflix's share fell from 8.7% to 7.7% over the same year (Nielsen). Huber Research estimates usage per Netflix member fell about 5%. HSBC notes YouTube's estimated $23B of 2026 creator payouts exceeds Netflix's roughly $20B cash content budget. Lower engagement limits price increases and raises churn risk. In our bear case, US price increases slow to 3–4% and US membership growth stops. That alone cuts about $13 from our target.

**Content costs and 2027 margins (high impact).** Wells Fargo rates the stock Sell, with a $57 target, citing 2027 margin risk after the content leadership change. Our base case grows content amortization 7.0% in 2027. Each 1 point of 2027 operating margin is worth about $560M of operating income. That is about $0.12 of 2027 EPS, or 3%. In our bear case, content amortization grows 10% in 2027 and operating margin falls to 30.4% by 2030.

**Advertising execution (medium impact).** Netflix has already missed its 2026 ad target once. Our base case needs ad revenue to grow 44% in 2027, to $3.9B. Our bear case assumes ad growth roughly halves, reaching $5.0B by 2030 against $6.9B in our base case. Our forecasts are already well below Bloomberg Intelligence and management's reported goal, which limits this risk.

**Cost of capital (medium impact).** Our DCF uses a 9.7% WACC. Bloomberg's estimate is 12.1%, because it uses a higher 8.1% equity risk premium. Each 0.5-point rise in WACC cuts our DCF value by about $5–6 per share. That takes about $3 off our target. At an 11.2% WACC, the DCF value falls to $52.30.

**Currency (medium impact).** About 56% of 2025 revenue came from outside UCAN. A 10% stronger US dollar against all other currencies would cut reported revenue by about 5–6%. Currency cut LATAM's 2025 reported growth by 9.6 points. We already deduct 3.5 points a year from LATAM growth.

**US content execution and leadership change (medium impact).** The chief content officer departed in 2026, and Peter Rice now leads content. A weak US slate would also hurt international regions, which rely on US titles that become global hits. This risk feeds directly into the engagement risk above.

**Capital allocation and M&A (low impact).** Netflix's attempt to buy Warner Bros. Discovery assets ended in February 2026, with Netflix receiving a $2.8B termination fee. A future large acquisition would divert cash from buybacks. Our model assumes 95% of free cash flow goes to buybacks.

**Balance sheet and refinancing (low impact).** Netflix has $15.2B of debt across 17 issues, with an average maturity of 4.4 years. Most maturities fall between 2027 and 2030. With A2 (Moody's) and A (S&P) ratings and $8.2B of cash, refinancing risk is low. We assume each maturity is refinanced at 5.5%. Netflix also had $24.0B of streaming content obligations at the end of 2025, much of it off the balance sheet (2025 Form 10-K). These commitments are larger than its debt, but they fund content our forecasts already include.

**Upside risks.** Our Buy rating could prove too cautious. Ad revenue reaching management's reported $9B goal, a stronger US slate under Peter Rice, or faster buybacks at today's price would each lift value. Our bull case combines these at $80.93. Short interest is low, at 2.1% of shares outstanding, so we see little sign of a large bet against the stock.
